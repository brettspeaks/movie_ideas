# Scene 4: The Subversion of Tragedy (The Unraveling)

## 1. The Warning & The Panic
* **The Ping:** After nearly a decade of radio silence, Lauren’s thread pings in the middle of a mundane family evening.
* **The Message:** `"Enjoy your time with Greg. Time is short."`
* **The Misinterpretation:** Lauren’s hyper-rational brain instantly catastrophic-predicts. As an ER physician, her mind jumps to the worst: sudden cardiac arrest, a fatal car crash, aggressive oncology. 

## 2. The Hyper-Vigilant Trap
* **The Overcorrection:** Driven by suffocating fear, Lauren transforms her marriage overnight. She stops treating Greg as "he'll do" and begins loving him with desperate, exhausting intensity.
* **The Paranoia:** Every delayed commute, every mild headache Greg gets, every late-night drive sends her into full medical panic mode. She checks his vitals, forces cardiac panels, and micromanages his life to prevent an imaginary disaster.
* **The Strained Marriage:** Greg feels suffocated and bewildered by her sudden, desperate intensity. The constant, unexplainable tension creates a quiet rift between them.

## 3. The Bombshell (The Twist)
* **The Breakpoint:** After two years of living in a state of permanent mourning, Lauren breaks down and texts the thread late at night: `"Is it today? Is this the day I lose him? Just tell me how he dies!"`
* **The Reply:**
  > `Don't be surprised when you receive the divorce papers. You and Greg had a fabulous run and you have two great kids. Spoiler alert: he doesn't pass away in a car crash in a few years.`
* **The Realization:** Future Lauren drops the brutal truth. Greg isn't going to die young. The relationship isn't destroyed by a tragic accident; it quietly dissolves years later because they simply grow into different people.

## 4. The Aftermath
* **The Emotional Shift:** The whiplash is immense—profound relief mixed with quiet heartbreak. 
* **The Core Insight:** Lauren realizes that `"Time is short"` wasn't a warning about a sudden, violent disaster. It was her older self reminding her not to waste the good years worrying about imaginary tragedies when the real thief of joy is just the gradual, quiet drift of everyday life.
