# Scene: Parallel Tables

INT. CLOSTER COMMUNITY CENTER - DAY (DECEMBER 2016)

Tinsel and mismatched Christmas ornaments line the walls of a cavernous, fluorescent-lit multi-purpose room. The hum of a commercial refrigerator vibrates through the floor. 

A large family holiday gathering is in full swing. Loud, chaotic conversations echo off the linoleum. 

Two long folding tables are pushed together, running parallel to each other. 

At the center of Table A sits FOSTER MOM (30s). She is actively managing the plates of the younger girls, cutting food, keeping tabs on the volume of the room. She looks grounded, built for this chaos.

Sitting directly opposite her, across the narrow divide at Table B, is BIOLOGICAL MOM (50s). She sits rigidly, holding a styrofoam cup of black coffee. She is watching the girls with a mixture of intense curiosity and profound detachment.

The noise of the room seems to drop away between them. They look across the gap.

BIOLOGICAL MOM
They look healthy. You've kept them together.

FOSTER MOM
(Without missing a beat, clearing a napkin)
That was the deal. We don't break up the loop.

BIOLOGICAL MOM
They don't have your eyes. But they have your posture. Sharp. Like you're waiting for the floor to drop.

FOSTER MOM
They aren't waiting for anything anymore. We built a floor. 

The ELDEST SISTER (10, non-related) slips into the seat next to Foster Mom, eyes tracking Biological Mom with a quiet, icy suspicion. She shifts her weight, subtly blocking the younger girls from view.

BIOLOGICAL MOM
(A faint, sad smile)
And you brought security. Smart. 

FOSTER MOM
We're just having lunch. Pass the rolls if they're on your side.

Biological Mom hesitates, then reaches across the physical boundary between the tables to slide the basket forward. Their fingers don't touch.

> **CRITIQUE & PROMPT NOTES:**
> * **The Good Concept:** Splitting them across two physical tables is a great visual metaphor. It forces them to look at each other while emphasizing the structural divide between their two worlds (the biological past vs. the fostered present).
> * **The Bad Cliché to Avoid:** Try to avoid making the biological mom an outright "villain" or making the interaction explosive right away. The tension is much heavier if it's quiet, polite, and restricted by the public setting of a family holiday.
