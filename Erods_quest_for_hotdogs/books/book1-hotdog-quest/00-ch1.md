# Chapter 1: Multi-Node Ingestion & Prehistoric Foraging

### Frame Story: Kitchen Table Telemetry
The coffee mug was still steaming. 

"So, how was work today, sweetie?" my mom asked, setting down a plate of toast.

"Oh, you know," I sighed, staring into the dark liquid. "Multi-node ingestion edge routes got completely cluttered because someone decided to inject unthrottled AI inference directly into our inbound queues. Total bottleneck."

She blinked, taking a slow sip. "...Is that why my Facebook wasn't loading?"

"Not quite. It’s more like... imagine an ancient engineer trying to survive an impending flood, but someone keeps dropping random data payloads directly into his path."

### Scene 1: The Gathering Storm (15,000 BCE)
Fifteen thousand years ago in the Pacific Scablands, Eron wasn't thinking about edge routes. He was thinking about time.

Days. Months. Maybe a year at best. 

Eron dug his calloused fingers into the damp earth, ripping up wild tubers with a frantic, methodical precision. As a master engineer of his tribe, he knew the structural integrity of the glacial ice dam holding back the massive lake to the east was failing. The subtle tremors in the basalt bed, the altered flow of the meltwater streams—the warning signs were everywhere. When that ice wall gave way, a wall of water hundreds of feet high would scour the entire valley clean, wiping his civilization off the map.

He was foraging vigorously, trying to build up emergency reserves for a evacuation plan that felt increasingly impossible. 

Then, his hand hit something cold. Not stone-cold—a strange, pulsing hum that vibrated straight up his forearm.

Eron cleared away the moss and mud. Resting in the dirt was a cluster of dense, petrified seed pods, fused together like heavy, metallic fasteners. They glowed with a faint, rhythmic bioluminescence, vibrating at a frequency that made the hair on his arms stand up.

*That's weird,* he thought, brushing off the remaining soil and balancing the heavy cluster in his palm. 

It didn't look natural. It didn't feel natural. It felt like... hardware.
